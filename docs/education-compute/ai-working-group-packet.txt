MILESTONES

October–November 2026 | Build the baseline
Confirm scope and resource owners; review campus routes; gather research, teaching and student perspectives.
Output: Versioned inventory, agreed input process and a list of unknowns.

November–end of Fall 2026 semester | Draft all four deliverables
Validate significant barriers; compare approaches; separate college actions from university dependencies.
Output: Preliminary inventory, needs assessment, portfolio and recommendations.

Early Spring 2027 | Refine and circulate within the college
Review drafts with faculty, students and resource owners; resolve corrections and agree priorities.
Output: Revised intra-college drafts with evidence, owners and unresolved questions.

As university discussions develop | Carry college priorities forward
Prepare the shared-service, policy, staffing and infrastructure questions that require university participation.
Output: College briefing for anticipated university-wide planning; timing not yet set.

USE AND REVIEW
The site does not collect needs responses. Agree an approved university channel, deliverable owner roles and review process. No group endorsements, college-wide needs totals, staff availability or funding are assumed. The separate pilot retains its own zero-cash/ten-hour constraints.

# Needs and gaps assessment

**Draft framework — inputs not yet collected by this site.** The resource inventory and historical provider changes identify questions to examine; they do not establish the size or distribution of college needs.

[Describe a need](needs.html) · [Download blank needs register](computing-needs-register.csv)

## Questions to investigate

| Area | What we need to learn | Useful evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Compute and cloud | Which activities need local devices, interactive sessions, batch jobs, GPUs or hosted models? | Small measured workloads, access delays and required concurrency |
| Software and models | Which licenses, APIs, environments and accessible interfaces are required? | Current agreements, use cases, compatibility and renewal terms |
| Data | Which datasets, rights, metadata, storage and preservation services are missing? | Data inventories, permissions, stewardship and export/restore needs |
| RSEs and support | Which skills and support windows are required, and what capacity is actually available? | Task estimates, existing commitments and service-owner confirmation |
| Research and instruction | How do research schedules, class deadlines and student access differ? | Course/project scenarios and faculty/student perspectives |
| Collaboration | What can be shared or improved before adding infrastructure? | Underused resources, compatible workflows and willing partners |

## Assess a reported gap

Distinguish **not available**, **not accessible**, **not known about**, **not supported**, **not suitable** and **not affordable**. They require different responses.

For each need, capture who is affected, the activity being blocked, current workaround, timing and evidence. Compare it with a verified resource route. A technical shortage should be distinguished from permissions, onboarding, skills or support barriers.

## Summarize responsibly

Report which parts of the college and which roles contributed, the number of usable inputs and important missing perspectives. Keep measured demand, self-reported needs, estimates and unanswered questions separate. Do not scale a few examples into college-wide GPU, spending or staffing totals.

## Ready for a preliminary draft when…

The group has a versioned needs register, a documented account of participation, significant gaps linked to evidence, and a clear list of remaining unknowns. Refine these with faculty, students and resource owners early in Spring 2027.


# Portfolio of approaches

**Candidates for investigation, not selected investments.** Combine approaches when useful; compare their support and recurring obligations as carefully as their technical benefits.

| Approach | Opportunity | First useful test | Main dependency |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Improve use of campus resources | Reduce duplicate purchases and onboarding friction | Map one research and one teaching workflow to documented campus routes | Eligibility, headroom, support and class-use terms |
| Shared RSE and technical support | Pool scarce expertise and reusable environments | Estimate a few tasks and trial a bounded support window | Named staff, existing commitments and funding; no new appointment assumed |
| Shared data and software stewardship | Reuse datasets, metadata and environments safely | Review a dataset/license and test a portable workflow | Rights, permissions, accessibility and renewal ownership |
| Allocation-backed compute service | Support larger CPU/GPU work without relying only on free tiers | Benchmark components and validate a target route | An award, operations capacity and provider terms; ACCESS pilot remains conditional |
| Efficient models and workflows | Lower unnecessary compute and improve reproducibility | Compare a smaller model, retrieval approach or checkpointed job against a quality baseline | Task-specific evaluation; savings must be measured |
| Partnerships and shared infrastructure | Connect compatible college, campus and external efforts | Define a small joint use case with an interested partner | Agreed responsibilities, access and sustainable support |
| Reusable teaching and evaluation tools | Avoid rebuilding assignment environments and grading workflows | Test exports and a synthetic class before adoption | Maintainer continuity, account terms and separate compute quotas |

## Use one comparison standard

For each candidate, document instructional/research benefit, users served, accessibility, data suitability, required effort, recurring costs, funding status, ownership and a reversible test. Do not rank options using invented cost savings or unmeasured capacity.

## Help prepare resource requests

The [needs tool](needs.html) produces a local request draft with documented resource routes and open questions. Copy or download its public/synthetic prompt for a fuller rewrite in **[U of A GenAI](https://responsibleai.arizona.edu/tools-support/u-gen-ai)**, which advertises no-cost NetID access with early-access and data restrictions. **[AI Verde](https://cyverse.org/ai-verde)** is an alternative after access and any token budget are confirmed. This site connects to neither model service. Review the text and share it yourself only under the chosen service’s data terms.

### Browser-model assessment

Browser inference works without API keys or per-request inference fees. We tested two quantized SmolLM2 models on synthetic examples in Chromium on this machine. The 135M model invented student-record details; a 360M model produced short drafts in about 9–13 seconds, but an integrated example added an unsupported participant condition and weakened unknown-hours language. Its initial model/runtime download was about 390 MB. These examples are a small feasibility check, not a general ranking of browser models.

The integrated 360M worker generated with networking blocked after loading. That establishes local execution for that test; it does not establish reliable drafting. We have kept browser inference disabled on the public site and chosen the simpler template-plus-campus-prompt workflow. The [SmolLM2 model card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct) also advises checking generated content. Preserve original quantities, source facts and unknowns in every draft.

A separate **GPT-6 Luna API evaluation** is prepared for three synthetic computing requests. It has not run and no API endpoint is connected to this site. API usage has token charges; testing does not establish an operating budget, institutional approval or a public service. [Official model documentation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna).


# Recommendations for review

**Draft action framework — priorities and endorsement pending.** The working group should refine these using the inventory and faculty/student needs, rather than assume a single platform solves the problem.

## Actions the college can pursue

1. **Maintain a concise resource inventory.** Identify an owner role for each entry and a review process. Publish access routes and limitations that resource owners confirm.
2. **Document needs across research and instruction.** Use consistent briefs and an agreed university channel; include faculty, students, RSEs and technical staff. Summarize participation and unknowns.
3. **Improve discoverability and reuse.** Test clear onboarding, reusable environments, shared documentation and relevant training before expanding infrastructure.
4. **Scope bounded pilots.** Match a small activity to a confirmed route and support window. Measure benefit, access, effort and recovery before making it required.

## Opportunities for university participation

| Shared question | College contribution | What needs university participation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Campus compute and hosted AI access | Concrete research/classroom scenarios and measured demand | Capacity, course access, account structure and service commitments |
| Software, APIs and data agreements | Priority tools, datasets, rights and renewal needs | Institutional agreements, permitted use and sustainable funding |
| RSE and technical support models | Skill/task estimates and existing college commitments | Shared staffing, escalation, training and long-term operations |
| Data governance and shared infrastructure | Workflow, preservation and collaboration requirements | Appropriate review, interoperability and institutional services |

## Before prioritizing an action

Attach the problem and evidence, users served, owner role, dependencies, effort, funding status, success measure and a review date. Explicitly distinguish an application, exploratory partnership or proposed role from a confirmed commitment.

The existing pilot’s **$0 cash and Greg’s 10 total hours/week** apply to that pilot. They are not a measured college budget or staffing ceiling. Do not assume funding or available staff for these broader recommendations.

# Run a working session

Use a short, repeatable review. This is a suggested meeting format, not a scheduled event or an assigned set of roles.

## Before the session

Choose a deliverable and review its current draft. Bring a public or appropriately shareable resource correction or needs brief. Use the group’s agreed university channel for internal material; the public website does not collect responses.

## Suggested 45-minute agenda

| Minutes | Activity | Record |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Confirm the decision or question | Scope and intended outcome |
| 5–15 | Review resources and access | Verified facts, owners and unknowns |
| 15–25 | Discuss needs and barriers | Research/teaching differences and missing perspectives |
| 25–35 | Compare promising approaches | Evidence, prerequisites and a useful small test |
| 35–45 | Agree next steps | Action, owner role, dependencies and review point |

## After the session

Update the draft’s date and status. Separate a decision from an open question or suggested action. Record who needs to confirm access, costs, support or permissions; do not list an unconfirmed resource as ready.

## Questions for the group to settle first

- Who maintains each deliverable and confirms factual corrections?
- Which approved university channel will receive needs briefs and internal notes?
- How will faculty, students and technical staff contribute, and how will missing perspectives be recorded?
- Who will review the preliminary drafts before early Spring 2027 circulation?
