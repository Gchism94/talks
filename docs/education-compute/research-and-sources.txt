Core problem: What we have previously relied on can no longer be guaranteed

Window: October 8, 2021 through October 8, 2026. FINDING
The evidence supports a continuity problem across multiple layers of teaching infrastructure: free compute and premium education benefits, student app hosting, and classroom orchestration. The selected history is not a measured estimate of total educational compute capacity, market prevalence, or learning losses. It does not establish that every free option has disappeared.

CLAIM BOUNDARIES
1. GitHub Classroom closed August 28, 2026; GitHub repositories, organizations and accounts remain. Allocation compute alone cannot replace Classroom workflows.
2. Colab Pro for Education closed to new sign-ups. Free Colab remains. Existing verified education subscriptions retain their remaining year under the FAQ conditions. No exact effective closure date is established. The limited offer was announced July 18, 2025. 'Quietly' describes the FAQ-based notice; the absence of another announcement is not proven.
3. AWS's new general Free Tier free plan lasts at most six months for accounts created July 15, 2025 onward. This is not a documented six-month reduction to all AWS student, Educate or Academy accounts. The new plan changes credits, eligible instances and spending protection as well as duration. Credits can outlast the free plan if the user upgrades to an eligible paid plan.
4. Heroku and Glitch were general-purpose services used in teaching, not education-only accounts. Replit's 2023 and 2024 milestones are stages of one withdrawal. Studio Lab is closed to new customers, not all existing customers.
5. Colab and Studio Lab appear in the timeline under the year they were observed. The deck does not manufacture an effective date from an undated current notice.

PROPOSED TEACHING SERVICE
Pool instructor demand into a justified ACCESS allocation structure agreed with the program and provider. Select a project type from measured need, rather than assuming that 'large' automatically requires Maximize. In parallel, assist eligible AI-course instructors with NAIRR Classroom requests. Operate a local service around the resources: browser labs, tested course environments, accounts and quotas, support, LMS/assignment/grading connections, and portable exports. These are proposed capabilities, not a deployment report or award confirmation.

The local program should offer instructor intake, benchmark assistance, proposal/biosketch preparation support, course rehearsals, cohort onboarding, primary technical support, usage/outcome evidence, midterm and final reports, and a plan for the next teaching cycle. NAIRR Classroom final reports are due within 30 days of course completion. No proposal has been submitted by this deck-building task.

ACCESS classroom periods generally follow courses, and classroom projects are typically not extended. NAIRR awards depend on resource match and availability and support U.S.-based AI instruction for U.S.-based participants. Both paths need future requests and capacity planning. Neither is a perpetual entitlement. A central service/umbrella allocation structure must be confirmed with the program and provider.

ILLUSTRATIVE CAPACITY MODEL
12 courses x 40 students = 480 course seats (not necessarily unique people).
CPU: 12 x 40 x 15 weeks x 8 core-hours/student/week = 57,600 core-hours.
With 25% allowance: 72,000 CPU core-hours.
GPU subset: 4 courses x 40 students x 6 weeks x 2 GPU-hours/student/week = 1,920 GPU-hours.
With 25% allowance: 2,400 GPU-hours.
CPU and GPU hours cannot be added or treated as ACCESS credits. Convert using the selected resource's accounting after benchmarks. GPU model, simultaneous access, memory, storage, idle use, licensing, security, accessibility, staffing and fallback operation need separate planning. No current allocation size or available quota was verified for this deck.

The proposed lead-in schedule begins 12 weeks before teaching; it is an operating target, not a promise of program review or provisioning speed. Rehearse at class concurrency and prepare an alternate lab before the first assignment. Keep code, environment definitions, data and assessment records portable. Institutionally supported staff time is a real cost even when students do not pay for compute.

PROVIDER CONSTRAINTS AND RECOMMENDATION METHOD
Codespaces is the preferred CPU baseline because its monthly entitlement is documented and portable dev containers support notebooks and code. Kaggle is the preferred free GPU supplement among the reviewed options because it offers notebook accelerators, with weekly quota and session limits. This is not a claim that it is universally best or more reliable than all alternatives. Free Colab remains a fallback; its variable quota and GPU availability cannot guarantee synchronous labs. Neither platform replaces a managed whole-class capacity commitment.

The mappings cover all three BS Information Science emphases and both MS Information Science subplans. Game and arts recommendations explicitly separate browser/device execution from cloud hosting. There is no complete free cloud workstation recommendation for heavy 3D / XR. ACCESS GPU desktops require software, licensing, device and latency validation; physical XR and installation work still needs campus equipment. PhD research belongs under research allocations, not a Classroom award merely because its user is a student.

Codabench is the intended benchmark platform, corrected October 11, 2026. It provides free competition hosting and a shared CPU scoring queue capped at 20 minutes; organizers supply code/GPU queues. cuda.live remains an independent optional micro-exercise route.

REPOSITORY CONTINUITY PLAN
Proposed repository program, not an existing campus service or completed migration. Start with two courses before scaling to all nine degree programs.

1 · Keep a viable free route
Use GitHub Free for ordinary Git hosting now. Assess an institution-sponsored GitLab for Education application for private class teams and additional features; annual renewal and use restrictions still apply.
Responsibility / timing: Instructor lead + college IT · before the next course

2 · Establish campus ownership
Inventory course repos, sizes, LFS, issues, wikis, artifacts and grading links. Give each course a responsible owner and successor. Keep submissions private; do not make student work public to escape a quota.
Responsibility / timing: Course owner + college IT · weeks 12–10

3 · Pilot a managed fallback
Evaluate a supported campus Git service first. If none fits, pilot Forgejo or self-managed GitLab on an institution-managed host with authentication, private projects, quotas and isolated grading runners. Budget staff, durable storage and recovery separately from compute awards.
Responsibility / timing: College IT + central IT · weeks 10–4

4 · Preserve more than Git
Take versioned nightly Git and LFS backups to independent storage; export issues, wikis, release files and course assessment links through supported paths. Define retention, access and deletion rules. A live mirror can copy deletions and cannot replace a separate backup.
Responsibility / timing: Named service operator · nightly; alert on failures

5 · Prove recovery, then scale
Before teaching, restore a private sample course on a second host and confirm history, LFS, permissions and grading links. Proposed pilot targets: at most 24 hours of data loss and recovery within one business day. Validate these targets before promising them.
Responsibility / timing: Operator + instructor · pre-term and after major changes

6 · Close the teaching cycle
Give students portable code exports, retain institutional assessment records under approved policy, and archive releasable research code/data with Libraries/ReDATA when appropriate. Review storage, restores, licenses and allocation renewal before the next cohort.
Responsibility / timing: Instructor + IT + Libraries · each term

Implementation boundary: Git/code mirrors do not preserve all forge or course metadata. LFS requires its own transfer; backed-up issues or assessment data need a usable export/restore design, not just a downloaded archive. Keep backups independent and versioned. Isolate execution of student grading jobs. ReDATA is for appropriate publicly releasable research outputs, not private submissions or the live course Git server. Retention periods must follow applicable institution policy; no arbitrary universal retention period is prescribed. Proposed 24-hour recovery-point and one-business-day recovery-time targets need a measured rehearsal. Operating infrastructure, storage and staffing need a college/IT commitment beyond time-limited ACCESS/NAIRR awards. No compute, GitLab or campus service request was submitted.

SYNTHESIS
The relevant ecosystem is a stack: public investment and grants; access programs and providers; teaching/application environments; data/metadata/workflows; object storage and delivery; identity, support, operations and preservation. Federal funding is not synonymous with all services being free, ready for a class, wholly federally financed or permanent. ACCESS providers, NRP/Nautilus and OSG/OSPool/OSDF must not be summed as independent capacity.

Many small jobs, not one big notebook
Add OSPool/PATh and checkpointed workflow execution for corpus processing, parameter sweeps and independent model evaluation.

Managed teaching environments
Map CloudBank Classroom, Jetstream2 course images, NRP hubs and eligible CyVerse apps separately from raw GPU allocations.

Inference as an API
NRP-hosted model APIs and requested NAIRR model/software access can support assignments without every student operating a GPU server.

Data-first services
MESA, NDP, FabAID, iDLab, BRIDGE and NSDF supply discovery, metadata, reusable workflows and cross-site access; their grants are not all compute accounts.

Object storage, caches and movement
OSN stores active data; OSDF/Pelican distributes it; Globus moves it. None automatically provides archive permanence or all operating costs.

Systems, networks and specialized hardware
Chameleon, CloudLab and FABRIC support systems/edge/distributed experiments and specialized hardware. Use only when the testbed purpose matches.

Campus delivery and staff funding
Open OnDemand, gateways, identity, orchestration, grant-funded facilitators and college operators make allocations teachable. Compute awards alone do not fund all of them.

Domain facilities and mixed funding
DOE, NASA and NIH add mission-specific routes. CC*/MRI can fund campus capability; AI Hubs and CyVerse/UArizona show why state, subscription and federal support need separate labels.

NIH AnVIL platform browsing is free but execution/storage/egress can cost money; it differs from Purdue ACCESS Anvil. CC*/MRI/SCIPE/CyberTraining are funding pathways, not current accounts or verified open calls. CloudBank GPU class capacity and IDSS production service guarantees were not established.

## Assess a reported gap

Distinguish **not available**, **not accessible**, **not known about**, **not supported**, **not suitable** and **not affordable**. They require different responses.

For each need, capture who is affected, the activity being blocked, current workaround, timing and evidence. Compare it with a verified resource route. A technical shortage should be distinguished from permissions, onboarding, skills or support barriers.

## Summarize responsibly

Report which parts of the college and which roles contributed, the number of usable inputs and important missing perspectives. Keep measured demand, self-reported needs, estimates and unanswered questions separate. Do not scale a few examples into college-wide GPU, spending or staffing totals.

# Primers for the working group

Six starting points for discussing computing resources across the **College of Information Science**. Use the questions to identify evidence and a useful next step.

## 1. Start with the activity

An interactive class, a batch research job, a hosted model and a public website need different services. A GPU is one component; memory, software, storage, accounts and support can be the actual constraint.

**Discuss:** What must people be able to do, when, and with how many simultaneous users?

[Develop a brief](needs.html) · [Optional technical estimate](compute-estimator.html)

## 2. Separate availability from dependable access

A free account or published machine specification does not reserve capacity or authorize classroom use. Research tolerates some queues; a scheduled class may not. Puma now permits class groups, but teaching and research share the PI allocation. Jobs may queue without deadline priority, and instructors support students.

**Discuss:** Who is eligible, what is guaranteed, and what happens when access fails?

[Resource inventory](inventory.html) · [Classroom checks](readiness.html)

## 3. Count the people and recurring work

Allocations and credits do not automatically supply research software engineers (RSEs), onboarding, accessible interfaces, course support or a backup operator. Estimate tasks and hours before proposing a staffing or funding model.

**Discuss:** Who maintains the service, supports users and handles renewals and recovery?

[Candidate approaches](approaches.html) · [Pilot hours and grant dependencies](budget.html)

## 4. Treat data, software and preservation separately

Code repositories, licensed datasets, active storage, databases and preservation repositories have different purposes. Check rights, permitted data, exports and restore. A working copy is not an independent backup or a scholarly archive.

**Discuss:** What may be stored or shared, who owns it, and how will it outlast the account?

[Repositories, hosting and data](repository-plan.html) · [Campus data and software services](inventory.html)

## 5. Compare routes by the whole commitment

Use existing campus services, shared infrastructure, efficient workflows and national allocations where they fit. Free tiers and promotional credits can support bounded experiments; an operating service also needs ownership, support and continuity.

**Discuss:** What small, reversible test would distinguish the promising options?

[Federal platforms](federal-compute.html) · [Cloud credits and limits](serverless-compute.html) · [Classroom 50](classroom50.html)

## 6. Keep the evidence proportional

A self-reported need, an estimate, a benchmark and an owner-confirmed commitment are different evidence. A few examples cannot establish college-wide demand, savings or available staffing.

**Discuss:** Whose perspective is missing, what is measured, and which claims still need confirmation?

[Needs assessment framework](needs-assessment.html) · [Report and limitations](report.html#evidence-and-limitations)

Record **the activity → the barrier → a route to investigate → a person who can confirm it → the next useful test**. Keep unknowns visible. The [working materials](supplemental.html#working-group-materials) provide the registers and review framework.

### Browser-model assessment

Browser inference works without API keys or per-request inference fees. We tested two quantized SmolLM2 models on synthetic examples in Chromium on this machine. The 135M model invented student-record details; a 360M model produced short drafts in about 9–13 seconds, but an integrated example added an unsupported participant condition and weakened unknown-hours language. Its initial model/runtime download was about 390 MB. These examples are a small feasibility check, not a general ranking of browser models.

The integrated 360M worker generated with networking blocked after loading. That establishes local execution for that test; it does not establish reliable drafting. Browser inference remains disabled on the public site. The local template, optional hosted Luna draft and manual campus prompt are separate routes. The [SmolLM2 model card](https://huggingface.co/HuggingFaceTB/SmolLM2-360M-Instruct) also advises checking generated content. Preserve original quantities, source facts and unknowns in every draft.

### GPT-6 Luna drafting test

On October 19, 2026, **GPT-6 Luna** completed three synthetic requests—data science instruction, MLIS data work and research software support—in **5.6–6.0 seconds each**. Review against supplied briefs and dated inventory facts found that all three preserved unknown costs, staffing, permissions and capacity. Drafts were 135–163 words and kept questions for resource owners separate.

**Assessment: useful first drafts with human review.** This was three examples with facts supplied, not a reliability guarantee or a matched comparison with browser models. Total usage was 4,157 input and 1,032 output tokens; estimated token charges were **about $0.001**, including reported cache writes, using [published standard rates](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-6-luna). This is an estimate, not a verified invoice. That initial test established no institutional approval or college operating budget. The subsequent optional integration has its own usage limits and deployment checks.
