Computing Resources Working Group¶

Computing for the College of Information Science. Understand what we have, identify what we need, and support research and teaching sustainably.

Working-group point person: Barney Maccabe, Associate Dean of Research · maccabe@arizona.edu.

Explore the inventory →Describe a need →Review the deliverables →

Our focus¶

Computing supports the full range of our college’s research and teaching: data science, AI and machine learning, library and information science, digital scholarship, games, extended reality and other information-centered work. The group considers hardware, software, data, cloud services, research software engineers (RSEs), technical support, collaboration and shared infrastructure across the college and university.

Growing demand, rising costs and uncertain funding make access and sustainable support important planning questions. Our focus is the needs, capabilities and perspectives of College of Information Science faculty and students. AI is one part of this broader computing remit.

01 / LANDSCAPEUnderstand current resources

Identify what is available, who can use it, and how it is supported and funded.

02 / NEEDSIdentify gaps and opportunities

Describe current and anticipated work, barriers and ways to improve use of what exists.

03 / APPROACHESExplore sustainable options

Compare technologies, shared services, partnerships, staffing models and innovation.

What we will produce¶

Deliverable Working draft
Resource inventory Access, costs, support, funding and limitations
Needs assessment Current and emerging needs, significant gaps and barriers
Portfolio of approaches Practical options for utilization, collaboration and innovation
Recommendations College actions and opportunities needing university participation

Timing: preliminary drafts of all four items by the end of the Fall 2026 semester; refinement and intra-college circulation early in Spring 2027. See milestones →

Start with college and campus resources¶

The inventory covers college IT and research labs, campus CPU/GPU computing, AI Verde, software, data and RSE support. Each entry identifies a contact and distinguishes documented terms from unanswered questions. Filter to College to review local capabilities and missing inventory details first.

Use this site¶

  • Faculty and students: describe your activity and barriers; technical estimates are optional.
  • Resource owners and support staff: review inventory entries and identify corrections or missing capacity.
  • Working-group members: use the working-session guide and draft deliverables.
  • College administrators: review proposed actions and university dependencies.
Working drafts. Public service documentation is a starting point, not confirmation of college access or spare capacity. No college-wide needs survey or group endorsement is claimed. The needs tool prepares a local brief; it does not submit or collect responses.

Supporting evidence and pilots¶

The provider-change report explains the continuity risks behind “What we have previously relied on can no longer be guaranteed.” The DS/MLIS compute pilot, ACCESS request and benchmarks are supporting proposals, not the working group’s predetermined solution.
